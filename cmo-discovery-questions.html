CMO Discovery Questions

Snowflake Sales Discovery Framework — Chief Marketing Officer Persona

The following 10 discovery questions are designed to uncover a CMO's key business challenges, desired outcomes, and success metrics. Each question maps to a Snowflake capability that addresses the underlying pain point. Use these to guide a consultative conversation, not as a rigid script.

Tip: Lead with the business challenge, not the technology. CMOs care about revenue, customer engagement, and speed to market — not data platform architecture. Let their answers guide you to the relevant Snowflake capability.
Question 1 — Customer 360
"How confident are you today that your team has a single, unified view of every customer across all your channels and touchpoints?"
Business Challenge: Customer data siloed across CRM, web analytics, email platforms, ad tech, and point-of-sale systems. Marketing teams make decisions on partial data.
Desired Outcome: A unified customer profile that combines first-party, second-party, and third-party data for hyper-personalised campaigns and journey orchestration.
Key Metrics: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), cross-sell/upsell conversion rate, campaign response rate by segment, data integration latency.
Snowflake Relevance: Snowflake as a single platform to consolidate all customer data; Snowpark for identity resolution; Marketplace for third-party data enrichment.
Question 2 — Marketing Attribution
"When you report marketing ROI to the board, how much of your spend can you confidently attribute to actual revenue?"
Business Challenge: Inability to connect marketing spend to revenue outcomes. Last-click attribution overstates some channels and undervalues others. Board loses trust in marketing numbers.
Desired Outcome: Multi-touch attribution model that accurately credits each channel and campaign, enabling confident budget reallocation.
Key Metrics: Return on Ad Spend (ROAS), Cost per Acquisition (CPA), attributed revenue by channel, marketing-sourced pipeline %.
Snowflake Relevance: Centralised data platform to join ad spend, CRM, and revenue data; Snowpark ML for custom attribution models; near-real-time data sharing with agency partners.
Question 3 — Speed to Insight
"How long does it take your team to go from 'we have a question' to 'we have a data-backed answer' — hours, days, or weeks?"
Business Challenge: Marketing analysts bottlenecked by slow queries, IT ticket queues for data access, and stitching together exports from multiple tools. Campaign windows close before insights arrive.
Desired Outcome: Self-service analytics where marketing teams can answer their own questions in minutes without waiting on engineering.
Key Metrics: Time to insight (hours → minutes), number of ad-hoc queries per analyst per week, analyst-to-IT request ratio.
Snowflake Relevance: Elastic compute (no queuing), governed data sharing across departments, Cortex Analyst for natural-language querying, Streamlit for self-service dashboards.
Question 4 — Personalisation at Scale
"How close are you to delivering truly personalised experiences to each customer, rather than broad segment-based campaigns?"
Business Challenge: Personalisation limited to basic segments (e.g. age, location). Unable to act on real-time behavioural signals. Competitors are delivering 1:1 experiences.
Desired Outcome: AI-driven micro-segmentation and real-time personalisation across email, web, app, and paid media.
Key Metrics: Engagement rate, conversion rate by personalisation tier, email click-through rate, average order value uplift.
Snowflake Relevance: Snowpark ML for propensity models and next-best-action; Cortex AI functions for sentiment and classification; Snowflake Marketplace for behavioural data enrichment.
Question 5 — Data Collaboration & Clean Rooms
"How are you currently measuring campaign overlap or shared audiences with your media and retail partners — and how much manual effort does that take?"
Business Challenge: Sharing customer data with partners for measurement or activation requires risky data exports, complex legal agreements, or expensive intermediaries. Privacy regulations make this harder every year.
Desired Outcome: Privacy-safe data collaboration with media partners, retailers, and publishers without moving or exposing raw data.
Key Metrics: Audience overlap %, incremental reach, partner co-marketing ROI, time to activate a partner data collaboration.
Snowflake Relevance: Snowflake Data Clean Rooms for privacy-safe audience overlap and measurement; Secure Data Sharing for zero-copy partner collaboration; Marketplace for data monetisation.
Question 6 — First-Party Data Strategy
"With third-party cookies going away and privacy regulations tightening, how prepared is your team to build a first-party data strategy that maintains your targeting effectiveness?"
Business Challenge: Dependence on third-party cookies and data brokers for audience targeting. GDPR, CCPA, and cookie deprecation threaten existing acquisition strategies.
Desired Outcome: A robust first-party data asset that powers targeting, measurement, and personalisation without reliance on third-party tracking.
Key Metrics: First-party data coverage (% of known customers), consent/opt-in rate, targeting match rate, cost-per-acquisition trend post-cookie.
Snowflake Relevance: Centralised consent-aware data platform; identity resolution via Snowpark; Clean Rooms for activation without data leakage; Marketplace for privacy-compliant enrichment.
Question 7 — Real-Time Campaign Optimisation
"If a campaign is underperforming right now, how quickly can your team detect it and make an adjustment — same day, next day, or next reporting cycle?"
Business Challenge: Campaign performance data arrives in batch (daily or weekly). By the time underperformance is detected, budget has been wasted. No ability to optimise in-flight.
Desired Outcome: Near-real-time campaign dashboards with automated alerts and the ability to adjust spend, creative, or targeting within hours.
Key Metrics: Data freshness (batch lag → minutes), budget waste recaptured, in-flight optimisation frequency, campaign pacing accuracy.
Snowflake Relevance: Snowpipe Streaming for near-real-time ingestion; Dynamic Tables for continuously refreshed campaign views; Cortex AI for anomaly detection; Streamlit for live dashboards.
Question 8 — Martech Stack Complexity
"How many marketing tools does your team use today, and what percentage of your team's time is spent moving data between them rather than acting on it?"
Business Challenge: Sprawling martech stack (average enterprise has 90+ tools). Each tool has its own data silo. Integration is fragile, expensive, and consumes analyst time.
Desired Outcome: A central data platform that serves as the single source of truth, with connectors to activate data in downstream tools without manual ETL.
Key Metrics: Number of data integration points, analyst time spent on data prep vs. analysis, ETL pipeline failure rate, total martech spend.
Snowflake Relevance: Openflow for native data ingestion; partner connectors (Fivetran, Airbyte); reverse ETL to push segments to ad platforms; single platform replacing point-to-point integrations.
Question 9 — AI-Powered Marketing
"Where are you on the journey of using AI and machine learning in your marketing — is it aspirational, experimental, or already driving decisions at scale?"
Business Challenge: AI/ML adoption blocked by data access issues, lack of ML infrastructure, or dependence on a small data science team. Models exist in notebooks but never reach production.
Desired Outcome: Production ML models for churn prediction, propensity scoring, content recommendation, and dynamic pricing — accessible to marketing users, not just data scientists.
Key Metrics: Number of ML models in production, prediction accuracy, churn reduction %, revenue uplift from AI-driven recommendations.
Snowflake Relevance: Cortex AI (built-in LLM functions, sentiment, classification); Snowpark ML for custom models; Cortex Agents for natural-language marketing analytics; Model Registry for MLOps.
Question 10 — Data Governance & Trust
"How often do different teams in your marketing org report different numbers for the same metric — and how much time is spent reconciling those differences?"
Business Challenge: Multiple versions of truth across teams. Campaign reports from the analytics team don't match the agency's numbers or the finance team's revenue figures. Trust in data erodes.
Desired Outcome: Governed, consistent metric definitions with role-based access — everyone sees the same numbers, with full lineage and audit trails.
Key Metrics: Time spent on data reconciliation, number of metric definition conflicts per quarter, data governance audit pass rate, time to onboard a new data consumer.
Snowflake Relevance: Snowflake Horizon (governance, lineage, classification, access policies); Semantic Views for consistent metric definitions; role-based access control; Universal Search for data discovery.
